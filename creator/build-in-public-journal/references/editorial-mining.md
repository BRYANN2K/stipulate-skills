# Qualitative editorial mining

Load this reference only after the user explicitly asks to mine private journal events or evaluate ideas for named channels. Do not perform editorial review during ordinary capture.

## Safety and truth first

Before considering channel fit, confirm qualitatively that the selected event is:

- supported by observed, author-supplied, or cited facts;
- potentially useful beyond a routine project status update;
- backed by an inspectable safe artifact when the central claim needs proof;
- free of secrets, customer/private data, personal detail, contract conflict, internal topology, active exploit detail, and unapproved quotation;
- clear about unresolved experiments, limitations, release state, and embargoes;
- specific enough to discuss without manufacturing drama.

If this boundary fails, use a truthful disposition such as `KEEP_PRIVATE`, `NOT_USEFUL`, or `NEEDS_EVIDENCE`. Do not compensate for weak evidence with a dramatic angle.

## Keep the idea bound to current evidence

Treat every idea as a derived view keyed to a stable source event. Record the event's current status and last dated update when mining. Before presenting an existing idea again, re-read the source event and its corrections/supersession links. If the source becomes pending, contradicted, rejected, superseded, embargoed, or less public-safe, revise the disposition immediately or rebind the idea to the verified superseding event. Do not preserve a candidate state from an older evidence snapshot.

## Evaluate only requested channels

The user may request one channel, several, or a channel not listed here. Evaluate only those named. Do not produce blank sections for unrequested channels and do not require a multi-channel card.

### X or another short-form text channel

A likely fit has a safe concrete observation, contrast, question, or artifact that remains accurate with brief context. It is a poor fit when compression would erase the limitation, proof, or necessary background. Stay at possible-subject and format level; do not write the opening line or thread.

### LinkedIn or another professional network

A likely fit has a real consequence for delivery, reliability, cost, quality, customers, team practice, or resource allocation, plus a reusable decision or process insight. It is a poor fit when the idea depends on performative vulnerability, generic leadership language, or an unproved business claim.

### Personal blog or long-form publication

A likely fit has enough method, chronology, alternatives, sources, limitations, or reproducibility for a durable explanation. It is a poor fit when one small fact would need filler to become an article or when private evidence cannot be shown safely.

For another requested channel, identify that channel's audience and content constraints without inventing platform rules or predicted performance.

## Qualitative candidate record

Keep only information that helps the author decide whether to write:

- stable source event ID, current status, and last update reviewed;
- possible subject and neutral angle, not a hook;
- audience utility;
- confirmed proof and missing evidence;
- limitations and claims not to overstate;
- questions only the author can answer;
- qualitative fit rationale and possible format for each requested channel;
- details to remove, generalize, delay, or keep private;
- disposition: `KEEP_PRIVATE`, `NOT_USEFUL`, `NEEDS_EVIDENCE`, or `CANDIDATE` with named channels.

Do not assign signal, privacy, virality, or platform scores; predict impressions; make a universal “viral” claim; or output finished copy. Different requested channels may need different angles, but the skill should not manufacture variants when one channel was requested.
